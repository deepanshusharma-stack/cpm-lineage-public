1The flow in one picture

The library has two entry points. diagnose(data) answers "what happened, and why". plan(data) answers "what should be done". Both are pure functions over plain dataclasses: no network, no database, and the clock is the latest date in your data, so the same input gives the same output.

Step Question Where it lives Entry point
1 Which of conversion, price or CPM moved? identity, decompose diagnose()
2 Which lever moved it? attribution, recommend diagnose()
3 Is a budget binding, a keyword bleeding, a CPM inflating? budget, cpm, keywords, geo diagnose()
4 What actions do the rules propose? actions.families plan()
5 Which proposals survive, in what order? actions.pipeline plan()
6 Ship it, wait, or hand it to a person? verdict plan()
7 Did the action do what the card predicted? store.ledger, outcomes you call it daily

An entity is the thing that holds a budget: a campaign, an ad group or a line item. A targeting unit is what you bid on inside it: a keyword, an audience, a category. Words such as "campaign" and "keyword" in card titles and finding names mean "entity" and "targeting unit".

2The data you feed in

The row contract

The only required feed is delivery: one PerfRow per entity and day, optionally split by targeting unit, geo and product.

Field Required Meaning Without it
date yes a datetime.date (not a string) the engine raises a TypeError
entity_id yes the budget-holding entity
spend yes money spent, your currency
impressions yes impressions served
sales for ROAS directly attributed revenue; defaults to 0 the entity cannot be judged on ROAS
units for ROAS attributed units or orders; defaults to 0 the identity cannot split conversion from price
targeting_unit, targeting_type no the keyword or audience, and its kind keyword blocks are skipped, with a note
geo no auction or reporting region no geo cards and no head-term pressure card (it needs a CPM rise in at least 3 geos)
product_id no the advertised product availability cannot be tied to an entity, unless Entity.product_ids is set
bid no bid in force that day the first read says "bid unknown"; the bid lever is not modelled
position no ad slot or rank position rules abstain
indirect_sales no halo or view-through sales entity ROAS is direct only
new_users no new-to-brand customers loss-making keywords are never spared as cheap acquisition
ad_type, status, segment, entity_name no labels unknown ad types get the fully levered default; unknown statuses read as live
clicks no clicks, where the platform has them nothing: no rule needs clicks

Additive fields (spend, impressions, sales, units, clicks, indirect_sales, new_users) are summed across rows. bid and position are rates and are only ever combined as weighted means. Hourly exports can be passed as they are: rows that repeat a date are summed.

The optional feeds

Feed Record Rows in the example Unlocks

A field typed X | None is optional everywhere. None means "the platform does not report this", and every consumer skips the dependent gate or says "not assessed". It is never read as zero.

The platform profile

Everything that differs between platforms is declared once in a PlatformProfile: the ad types and what each can do, the status vocabulary, what position means, the geo grain, the auction type, the trading-day hours and a money scale. Section 10 covers it.

Step1The identity tells you which channel moved

QuestionROAS moved between two windows. Was it conversion, price, or the cost of an impression?

Formula. On a platform billed per thousand impressions the impression count cancels, so ROAS factors into three channels with no remainder:

ROAS = sales / spend = ICR1k x ASP / CPM

ICR1k = 1000 x units / impressions     conversions per thousand impressions
ASP   = sales / units                  average selling price
CPM   = 1000 x spend / impressions     cost per thousand impressions

d ln ROAS = d ln ICR + d ln ASP - d ln CPM          (exact, residual 0)

ICR (impression conversion rate) stands in for the click funnel, because many CPM platforms report no clicks. Where clicks exist, ICR = CTR x CVR and nothing else changes. A log-point is one unit of the natural-log change; for small moves 0.01 log-points is about 1%.

What the output looks like. Diagnosis.layer1 for the account, and EntityDiagnosis.layer1 for each entity:

account ROAS 3.0433 -> 2.7945   ln ratio -0.0853
  icr1k      1.242 ->     1.196   signed log-points -0.0376   share of move +44.1%
  asp      258.416 ->   266.829   signed log-points +0.0320   share of move -37.6%
  cpm      105.440 ->   114.184   signed log-points -0.0797   share of move +93.4%
  sum of log-points -0.0853   exact=True
first read: code=self_inflicted_bid  bid change +18.8%
cmp-generic: ln ratio -0.2188 = icr1k +0.0231  asp -0.0062  cpm -0.2356

Read it like this. Account ROAS fell 8.2%. Conversion cost 3.8 log-points, a higher selling price gave back 3.2, and CPM cost 8.0. The contributions are signed so that a CPM rise shows as a negative number, and they sum to the ROAS move exactly. "Share of move" divides each contribution by the total, so a channel that pushed against the move has a negative share.

The first read. layer1_verdict turns the table into one of six codes, with no model:

Condition (on a ROAS drop) Code
CPM is the worst channel and the bid moved less than 7% auction_pressure
CPM is the worst channel and the bid rose 7% or more self_inflicted_bid
CPM is the worst channel and the bid fell 7% or more cpm_rose_bid_cut
CPM is the worst channel and there is no bid data cpm_rose_bid_unknown
ICR is the worst channel conversion_fell
otherwise asp_fell

In the example the spend-weighted bid rose 18.8%, so the first read is self_inflicted_bid. Keep that in mind for step 3, where the keyword-level classifier, which also sees share of voice, names a rival as the cause. The first read is cheap and sees only delivery rows. Treat it as a pointer.

Also in this step. ad_type_shift_share splits the account move into mix (spend shifted between ad types) and rate (ROAS changed inside a type) with the exact midpoint formula mix_i = (w_c - w_p)(r_c + r_p)/2, rate_i = (w_c + w_p)/2 x (r_c - r_p). pareto_drag_rows ranks the entities that pull blended ROAS down. Here the move is rate-led (-0.284 against +0.035), and two entities carry all of the drag.

Thresholds that matter (IdentityConfig, OrchestrationConfig):

Field Default Effect
OrchestrationConfig.window_days 30 the comparison is the last 30 days against the 30 before
IdentityConfig.verdict_bid_flat_pct 7.0 a bid change under 7% counts as flat
IdentityConfig.verdict_min_spend 500 no first read below this window spend (reference money)
IdentityConfig.min_dln 1e-4 below this move the share denominator falls back

How to change them. The window: diagnose(data, options=OrchestrationConfig(window_days=14)), or pass windows= built with trailing_windows, resolve_window or a WindowPair of your own. The identity thresholds are arguments of the functions themselves: layer1_verdict(result, cfg=IdentityConfig(verdict_bid_flat_pct=10)).

Guard. When the prior window holds no delivery, the report says "no comparison window" instead of decomposing a move from zero. When any channel is zero in either window the result is marked exact=False and no first read is given.

Step2Attribution names the lever

QuestionThe identity said which channel moved. Which thing that changed explains it: the bid, the position, share of voice, availability, price?

Method in one line. For each entity, regress the log of each channel on daily lever values, then credit each lever with coefficient x (mean in the current window - mean in the prior window):

contribution[lever] = sum over channels of  sign(channel) x w[channel][lever] x (z_current - z_prior)

A lever is one entity-by-day number. There are eleven. Eight are things the advertiser controls (tier 1): bid, position, own_paid_sov, own_organic_sov, own_availability, competitor_share (spend share on competitor terms), own_discount, price_gap. Three are outside conditions (tier 2) kept in the model as controls and never turned into an action: log_category_impressions, comp_availability, comp_discount.

Two models are fitted, and they have to agree:

  • The level model is a ridge regression with sign constraints (a higher bid cannot lower CPM), shrunk toward account-wide elasticities fitted over all entities.
  • The day-to-day model is a second ridge on first differences. It is right only when daily changes in a lever line up with daily changes in the channel, so it is not fooled by slow trends.

The jury compares them lever by lever. Both material (at least 5% of the move) with the same sign is confirm; opposite signs is contest; only the level model material is solo.

The verdict. One label for the whole attribution, first match wins:

Rule Verdict
too few model days, or fewer than four live levers thin-data
ROAS barely moved while levers offset each other, or the levers explain under 20% of the move unexplained
the two models disagree on the direction of the largest lever unexplained
the day-to-day model predicts clearly worse than "tomorrow equals today" (skill below -0.25) unexplained
the largest lever is confirm and day-to-day skill is at least 0 confirmed
otherwise directional

What the output looks like. EntityDiagnosis.attribution for cmp-generic:

confidence={'level': 'High', 'days': 60, 'imputed': 0.0, 'skill': 0.353}
explained: net 99.9%  gross 102.8%  residual 0.1%  basis=move

bid                           86%      -0.1885     -0.2046  confirm  137.336 -> 194.957
own_paid_sov                  13%      -0.0284     -0.0484  confirm  0.350 -> 0.303

own_organic_sov               -1%       0.0018      0.0045  None     0.301 -> 0.300
own_availability               0%      -0.0005      0.0014  None     0.950 -> 0.947

The entity's ROAS fell 0.219 log-points. The bid, up from 137 to 195, accounts for 86% of that and both models agree. Paid share of voice slipping accounts for 13%. The two discount levers were folded into price_gap by the collinearity guard, and competitor_share had no coverage.

recommend() then turns the attribution into at most three actions for the entity. For cmp-generic it returns "Cap bids on protein bar, healthy snack", "Raise bids on energy bar" and an availability action whose target list is empty in this run.

Thresholds that matter (AttributionConfig, TierConfig):

Field Default Effect
TierConfig.mature_min_days 21 fewer model days: thin-data, no entity fit
TierConfig.full_min_days 35 from here the pooling strength is chosen by cross-validation
thin_min_levers 4 fewer live levers: thin-data
coverage_min 0.20 a lever is live only with real values on more than this share of days
jury_materiality 0.05 a model votes on a lever only at 5% of the move
explained_floor_pct 20 below this explained share the verdict is unexplained
skill_floor -0.25 below this day-to-day skill the verdict is unexplained
confirmed_min_skill 0.0 confirmed needs at least this skill

How to change them. diagnose() passes EngineConfig.tiers and EngineConfig.position through, so diagnose(data, config=EngineConfig(tiers=TierConfig(mature_min_days=28))) works. The AttributionConfig fields are not threaded through diagnose(). To change them, call the function yourself: attribution.attribute(panel, windows, cfg=AttributionConfig(...)).

What it needs. Measured on the example: with only the delivery feed, or with the delivery and budget feeds but neither the share-of-voice nor the shelf feed, every entity returns thin-data, because fewer than four levers have coverage. With either of those two feeds the attribution runs.

Limit. The attribution is observational. "Confirmed" means two regressions agree on the largest lever and the day-to-day one is no worse than a naive forecast. It is not a causal estimate, and a lever that is not in the model (creative, a competitor's stock-out you do not observe) cannot be named.

Step3Budget, keyword and CPM checks

These checks do not depend on the attribution model. Each produces typed Finding rows, and each finding names the module whose numbers it carries (Finding.source).

Budget: is the cap binding, and is a raise worth it?

QuestionDoes this entity run out of daily budget, and would the next unit of budget return more than it costs?

Formulas.

capped day      spend >= 0.9 x daily budget
capped entity   at least 7 spending days, and capped on >= 60% of them
curve           sales = b x ln(1 + spend / theta)        fitted per entity on about 90 days
marginal ROAS   b / (theta + spend)                      what the next unit of spend returns

probe_budget collects the facts and raise_verdict turns them into one verb. First match wins: ended status, unknown ROAS (never raise blind), a day-parted schedule, blocked availability, 40% or more of spend below breakeven, a flat or past-ceiling curve, a mostly organic entity, and only then a raise. That ladder applies at or above the scale bar (2 times breakeven by default). Between breakeven and the scale bar the verb is "hold and monitor"; below breakeven it is "tighten". A raise needs a marginal ROAS of at least 1.0 at the cap. Between 0.9 and 1.0 the verdict is a hold.

What the output looks like.

budget probe cmp-winner: cap 6,350/day, avg spend 6,350, capped 30 of 30 days,
    exhausts at hour 11.3
  marginal ROAS at the cap 2.80 from the account-pooled curve (r2 0.34);
    own curve not usable: daily spend barely varies (cv 0.00002)
  raise verdict: code=top_up  verb='Top up daily budget'  range 8,255-12,700/day
    estimator=saturation_curve

This is worth reading closely, because it shows the curve's weak point. An entity that hits its cap every day spends the same amount every day, so its own curve cannot be fitted (spend that never varies reveals no response). The probe falls back to a curve pooled over every entity's days, which may lend a marginal ROAS but never a ceiling. The recommended range is then the plain multiples 1.3x to 2.0x of the cap, with 1.5x as the recommended point.

Thresholds that matter (BudgetConfig, BudgetBoundPolicy):

Field Default Effect
cap_hit_fraction 0.9 a day is capped at 90% of the cap
capped_day_share 0.6 the cap binds when capped on 60% of active days
sat_min_cv 0.15 spend variation an own curve needs
sat_decision_r2 0.3 only fits at or above this may block a raise or size a ceiling
marginal_ceiling 1.0 a raise needs this marginal ROAS at the cap
marginal_block 0.9 below this the wording is "blocked", not "hold"
range_low_mult / range_rec_mult / range_high_mult 1.3 / 1.5 / 2.0 the recommended range
BudgetBoundPolicy.early_exhaust_minutes 720 "runs out early" means before this minute of the day

How to change them. The budget-bound policy and trading day ride on EngineConfig: EngineConfig(budget_bound=BudgetBoundPolicy(early_exhaust_minutes=900)). BudgetConfig is not threaded through diagnose(); call budget.probe_budget(..., cfg=BudgetConfig(...)) directly.

CPM: why did the price of an impression rise?

QuestionA keyword's CPM rose. Did we buy a better spot, did a rival push in, or did the slot get dearer?

Rule. classify_cpm_move explains only rises above 5%, for the top four keywords by spend. First match wins:

Evidence Kind
position improved by 0.3 slots or more, or own ad share rose 5 points or more bought position
a rival's ad share rose 3 points or more rival pressure
position worsened by 0.3 slots or more worse spot
none of these auction price

Output.

CPM move 'protein bar': 149.2 -> 241.5 (+62%)  kind=rival pressure
    position delta -0.05  own SOV delta -0.12  rival rival-b gain +0.23

Position did not move, own share of voice fell 12 points and rival-b gained 23 points, so the kind is rival pressure. This is the planted event: the generator raised the bid and CPM together on this keyword while a rival's ad impressions rose 2.5 times. The account-level first read in step 1 called the same move "self-inflicted", because the bid did rise and it cannot see share of voice. Without a MarketRow feed the classifier has no rival evidence and would say "auction price".

Thresholds (CpmConfig): explain_min_rise 0.05, explain_position_step 0.3, explain_sov_gain 0.05, explain_rival_gain 0.03, top_n 4. Change them with diagnose(data, cpm_cfg=CpmConfig(top_n=10)).

Keywords: what is losing money?

Rule. cut_recs flags a keyword when its window spend is above max(8,000 reference money, 3% of scope spend) and its ROAS is below breakeven, unless it buys new customers more cheaply than the median of its role.

keyword cut: 'rivalb bar' role=competitor spend 142,904 ROAS 0.34 verdict=cut
    (ROAS 0.34 is below 1.00 on material spend (142904 > floor 51215))

keyword_leaks runs the same idea across every entity of a segment, so a keyword split over several campaigns is judged on its combined money. Thresholds are in KeywordConfig (cut_floor_ref, cut_floor_share, cut_roas_be_mult).

Collisions: are two of your own campaigns bidding against each other?

Rule. find_collisions groups rows by keyword and geo, keeps groups with at least two live campaigns that each spent 10,000 reference money or more and advertise an overlapping product, and names the campaign with the best ROAS as the keeper. The rest of the spend is the waste.

Availability: did stock fall?

Rule. geo_drill compares own availability per geo between the two windows. A fall of 0.05 or more becomes a finding, and a product whose own fall in that geo is 0.10 or more is named.

[availability_drop] availability of sku-a2 in geo-south fell 0.95 -> 0.58

The planted drop is to 0.45 for the last 21 days. The finding reports 0.58 because it is the mean over the 30-day window. A window average always understates a recent step.

Guards on findings. Keyword-cut and collision findings need 7 delivery days in the current window (OrchestrationConfig.min_finding_days). At most 10 findings per kind are reported.

Step4Rule families propose cards

QuestionGiven the window aggregates, which actions do the rules propose?

A rule family is a pure function from an ActionContext (window sums per entity, keyword, segment and geo) to a list of ActionCard. A card is one proposed action: a lever, a direction, a size, the trigger in words and numbers, and a stake. Families only propose. They do not veto, rank or cap; that is step 5.

Family Proposes Lever
efficiency budget cut on a loss-maker, budget raise on a capped winner, keyword pause budget, keyword
growth ride a growing segment with its best campaign; add converting keywords budget, keyword
defense detect new entrants; fund the brand campaign when a rival takes share budget, watch
bid raise bids on unseen converters, lower them on visible bleeders, hold or yield a head term bid
experiment a day-part test where the answer is uncertain experiment
sov-gap close a gap between market share and ad share of voice budget, watch
geo split out a weak geography; expand into an under-penetrated one geo
collision resolve an own-versus-own keyword collision keyword

There are three rule profiles. classic uses absolute ROAS bands relative to breakeven. verdict uses one relative threshold per keyword role and a ladder of bands. marginal is classic plus a later re-sizing of budget cards on finite-difference marginal ROAS.

Example formulas (the ones that fired below):

classic raise    ROAS in [3 x be, 12 x be), spend >= 10,000, budget-bound
                 pct = clamp(round(100 x (target hour - exhaust hour) / exhaust hour x 0.9), 5, 50)
keyword pause    conquest term, spend >= 10,000, ROAS below min(role floor, be)
head-term hold   biggest keyword, spend >= 50,000, CPM up > 10% in >= 3 geos, ROAS above the floor
collision        stake = 15% of the spend that sits on the weaker campaign

be is the effective breakeven, max(breakeven / max(ltv_mult, 0.1), 0.1). With the default economics it is 1.0, so "3 x be" reads as ROAS 3.

What the output looks like. The raw candidates, before any gate:

efficiency-scale budget   Increase Budget +50% (6,350 -> 9,525)   [cmp-winner]
    why: ROAS 6.30x on 190,500 spend and the daily budget runs out early (utilisation 100%)
    do:  raise 50%; hold if marginal ROAS decays below 2.00x          stake 476,249
efficiency-cut   keyword  Pause / Negative Keywords               [cmp-conquest: rivalb bar]
    why: 1 keyword(s) lose money: rivalb bar (0.34x)                  stake 93,944
bid              bid      Hold Bid Under Pressure                 [cmp-generic: protein bar]
    why: CPM on 'protein bar' rose more than 10% in 4 geos; ROAS 2.67x still clears 1.00x
    do:  hold the bid; the term can afford the fight                  stake 45,427
collision        keyword  Resolve Keyword Collision               [cmp-overlap: energy bar]
    why: 2 own campaigns bid 'energy bar' in geo-north; 130,292 sits on the weaker ones
    do:  keep it on cmp-generic; negative-match it elsewhere          stake 19,544

Thresholds that matter (ActionsConfig, 123 fields; the ones behind these four cards): scale_min_mult 3.0, scale_max_mult 12.0, scale_min_spend 10,000, scale_pct_max 50, pause_conquest_min_spend 10,000, head_min_spend 50,000, head_cpm_rise 1.10, head_min_geos 3, collision_min_spend 10,000.

How to change them.

from dataclasses import replace
from cpm_lineage import ActionsConfig, plan

plan(data, actions_cfg=replace(ActionsConfig(), scale_min_mult=2.5, head_min_geos=2))
plan(data, rule_profile="verdict")            # another profile
plan(data, families=["efficiency", "bid"])    # a subset of families

Step5The pipeline resolves conflicts and caps the plan

QuestionWhich candidate cards may a person act on, in what order, and how sure is the engine?

The order is fixed. Each stage either removes a card, changes its status, or annotates it.

Stage What it does One-line rule
holdout leaves a stable 15% of eligible entities untouched each month as a comparison group held when sha1(entity + "|" + period)[:8] mod 100 < 15
scrub removes cards on held-out or dead entities
vetoes under a running experiment; a raise that is not budget-bound or cannot be stated; a lever the ad type lacks status becomes vetoed
conflict table supply gate, duplicate merge, bid conflicts, budget conflicts highest family priority wins
scoring confidence and expected value for every card see below
guardrail one run-level check: a ROAS floor or a spend ceiling raises below the floor are vetoed
equity gate no cuts on the brand's own-term campaign while the brand loses share
caps per family and lever, then 25 pending cards overall
waves cuts first, bid moves and tests second, raises third so outcomes stay readable

Conflict table. When two cards contradict each other on one entity's budget, the family with the higher priority wins and the loser becomes superseded:

defense 4.0 > supply 3.5 > efficiency-cut 3.0 > collision 2.5 > growth 2.0 = sov-gap 2.0
        > geo 1.8 > efficiency-scale 1.5 > bid 1.2 > experiment 1.0

There is one exception. If the losing side carries more than 3 times the winner's stake (stake_ratio_valve), the table abstains and the whole group becomes a tension group: all cards stay visible, and the ledger lets a person apply exactly one.

Confidence. A bounded sum of labelled parts, so you can re-derive it by hand:

confidence = clamp(40 + evidence window + spend depth + effect clarity or card type
                      + availability + young entity + realised win rate + precedent, 25, 90)

For the raise on cmp-winner: 40 + 15 (30-day evidence window) + 9 (spend depth) + 15 (effect clarity: far from breakeven) = 79.

Expected value. For a budget card, est = budget change x ROAS per day for a raise and |budget change| x max(1 - ROAS, 0) for a cut. ev = est x p, where p is the family's realised win rate once the ledger holds five paired outcomes, and 0.5 before that.

Three things to notice.

  1. The holdout hides a planted problem. In the default run cmp-conquest is held out, so the bleeding keyword on it gets no card. That is the holdout working as designed, and it surprises first-time readers. Turn it off with PipelineConfig(holdout_pct=0) or name the group with plan(data, holdout=[...]).
  2. No pipeline gate fires on the default run. The synthetic account has no conflicting cards. The third run shows a gate firing: the run check vetoes the raise and the card stays visible with its reason. A run check also raises the floor the families judge against, which is why the hold on 'protein bar' became a yield and a collision card became a bid cut.
  3. A vetoed card is kept. Vetoed and superseded cards stay in Plan.actions with a status and a reason. The only cards removed are merged duplicates, cards on held-out or dead entities, and pending cards beyond a reviewability cap.

Limit. The card's expected value is computed on the size the family asked for. In the example est_day is 19,996 (3,175 extra budget a day at ROAS 6.30), which is the +50% ask. Step 6 then trims the raise to +25% and the expected value is not recomputed. Read est_day together with change.pct_requested.

Step6Verdicts

QuestionFor each card: ship it, wait, or hand it to a person?

Every card leaves with verdict = {"state", "why", "lane"}. Not acting is recorded, never silent.

State Meaning Lanes
ACT ship it ACT
OBSERVE the read is not trustworthy yet quarantine, bad-budget-field, insufficient, below-bar, unfillable
HOLD the read is fine but something time-bound must pass launch-grace, price-event, monitoring-window
ESCALATE a person must decide automation-owned
NO-ACTION already decided elsewhere conflict, gate, partitioned, or the card's status

The verdict pass runs in a fixed order: shaping and sweeps first, then the gates (Figure 4).

Shaping (before the gates).

  • Bid-down before pause. A keyword pause is retyped to a -25% bid cut, re-read in 5 days. The pause ships only if the re-read still fails. The cheapest reversible lever goes first.
  • Staircase. A budget raise above 25% is trimmed to 25%, re-read in 7 days. The original ask is kept in change.pct_requested.
  • Sweeps. For entities that received no card: a cumulative-loss sweep (loss of at least 2% of book spend), a freeze on an unowned spend ramp, "winner went dark", "justify or kill" for spend on an ad type with no attributed sales (at least 50,000), and bounded probes for entities stuck under the evidence floor.

The gates (walk order). Integrity (phantom one-day cohorts, placeholder budgets), lifecycle (no cuts in the first 5 days), evidence (at least 1,000 window impressions), price event (a discount swing of 8 points holds media verdicts), action lock (one open action per entity for 7 days), automation (do not fight a bidder that owns the lever), risk bars, terminals.

Every gate is inert without its input. plan() supplies telemetry, the applied-action history and the profile's ad-type capabilities. It does not supply a discount index or an automation-owner map, so the price-event and automation gates never fire from plan(). To use them, build a verdict.SpineContext with price_index and automation and call verdict.run_spine on the cards from plan(data, spine=False).

Risk bars. The confidence a card must reach depends on how hard the action is to undo:

watch 25 < restore / freeze 30 = experiment 30 < bid 45 < budget raise 55 < any cut 65
plus 10 on every bar while the whole book is younger than 21 live days

What the output looks like.

-- classic: {'ACT': 5}

   ACT                  Decrease Bid (first response)      [cmp-conquest] ladder=bid-down-first

In the classic run the raise was trimmed by the staircase, the keyword pause became a bid cut, and a sweep added a "justify or kill" card for the reach campaign, whose spend has no attributed sales. Thresholds that matter (VerdictConfig): bar_cut 65, bar_budget_up 55, bar_bid 45, launch_grace_days 5, evidence_min_impressions 1,000, lock_days 7, staircase_step_pct 25, ladder_bid_step_pct -25, bleeder_book_share 0.02, untracked_min_spend 50,000.

How to change them. plan(data, verdict_cfg=VerdictConfig(bar_cut=70, staircase_step_pct=15)). plan(data, spine=False) skips the verdict pass and returns the pipeline's cards untyped.

What the spine needs from you. The action lock, the bid-ladder memory and ownership read plan(..., applied=[...]), a list of AppliedAction. Get it from the ledger with ledger.applied_actions(account, platform). Without it those gates are inert: the spine fails open.

Step7The ledger scores what happened

QuestionDid an applied action do what its card predicted?

plan(data, ledger=ActionLedger(), account="...") saves the plan and writes one ledger row per card and horizon (7, 14 and 30 days), including the cards that were vetoed or lost a conflict. You record decisions with ledger.mark(...), and once a day you call pair_outcomes(...).

Formula.

before = [d0 - h, d0)      after = [d0, d0 + h)      d0 = the day the action was applied
tide   = segment sales in the after window / segment sales in the before window
expect = ROAS before x clamp(sqrt(tide), 0.5, 2.0)

The tide is how the whole market moved. The square root damps it: a market running twice as fast raises the expectation by about 1.4 times, not 2.

Direction On Track when Otherwise
raise ROAS after is at least 85% of the expectation Off Track
cut ROAS after is at least 95% of the expectation and at least 60% of revenue is kept Review
watch, hold, freeze ROAS after is within 15% of the expectation Review

The asymmetry is deliberate. A cut that misses is Review, because the money was saved either way. A raise that misses is Off Track, because money was spent. Other labels: Too early (before day 3), No signal (no spend on one side), Unreadable (another action landed on the same entity inside the window). Cards that were not taken are scored as counterfactuals (cf-right, cf-wrong, cf-nosignal), which is how the priority table itself can be audited.

Check one row by hand. For cmp-overlap: expectation 1.757 x sqrt(1.027) = 1.780, the cut bar is 0.95 x 1.780 = 1.691, and ROAS after is 1.656, so the label is Review.

Limit of this example. The synthetic data does not react when a card is marked applied. These labels describe each entity's untreated trajectory. They show the mechanics, not that the actions worked.

Three details that matter in practice:

  • The ledger measures ROAS on direct sales (sales / spend). The plan's entity ROAS counts indirect sales by default, which is why cmp-winner reads 6.30 in the plan and 5.25 here.
  • Only the 7-day horizon feeds confidence, and only once a family has 5 paired outcomes (PipelineConfig.winrate_min_n). The win rates above, with n = 1, change nothing yet.
  • A row is paired once and then never changes. Pairing waits until the feed covers the whole after window.

Thresholds that matter (OutcomeConfig, LedgerConfig): raise_hold 0.85, cut_hold 0.95, revenue_retention 0.60, flat_band 0.15, too_early_days 3, horizons (7, 14, 30), win_rate_horizon 7. Change them with pair_outcomes(..., cfg=OutcomeConfig(raise_hold=0.9)) and ActionLedger(cfg=LedgerConfig(horizons=(7, 28))).

The loop, in code.

from cpm_lineage import plan
from cpm_lineage.store import ActionLedger, SQLiteBackend
from cpm_lineage.outcomes import pair_outcomes

ledger = ActionLedger(SQLiteBackend("ledger.sqlite"))
name = data.profile.name

p = plan(data, ledger=ledger, account="acct-1",
         applied=ledger.applied_actions("acct-1", name))      # arms locks and cooldowns
ledger.mark("acct-1", name, p.id, p.actions[0].id, "applied")  # an operator decision
pair_outcomes(ledger, "acct-1", name, data.perf,               # run once a day
              today=data.latest_date, offtake=data.offtake)

A second loop, outcomes.evaluate, gives a difference-in-differences read of a logged recommendation (the entity's ROAS change minus the same change over the rest of the account) and checks from the change log whether the change was actually made.

The adapter contract

An adapter is anything with a profile and a perf_rows() method. In practice you build a Dataset (a profile plus lists of records) and pass it to diagnose() and plan(). Three ready routes:

Route Use when
CsvAdapter(path, column_map, profile, ...) you have exports as files
DataFrameAdapter(frame, column_map, profile, ...) you already hold a frame or a list of dicts
subclass BaseAdapter and fill self.dataset the source is an API with nested records

examples/bring_your_own_data.py shows the first and examples/custom_platform_adapter.py the third, on an invented platform that has clicks, a second-price auction, no position field and country geos. docs/adapter_guide.md is the step-by-step version of this section.

Checklist: what your platform must provide

Required

  • One row per entity and day with date (a datetime.date), entity_id, spend, impressions.
  • sales and units for anything you want judged on ROAS.
  • A PlatformProfile with at least a name. The defaults describe a single-auction CPM platform with no clicks, a 1-is-best position and an unknown auction type.

Declare, because a wrong default changes decisions

  • status_map: every raw status mapped to a canonical one. Unmapped strings fall back to a small generic vocabulary, and unknown strings read as live.
  • ad_types: one AdTypeCapability per ad type. Mark types without attributed sales sales_attributed=False, or they will be judged on a ROAS of zero.
  • currency_unit: the money thresholds assume material campaigns spend thousands of currency units a day. If yours spend 50 a day, set 0.01.
  • position: PositionSemantics(available=False) if there is no position field, and lower_is_better=False if a higher number is better.
  • auction_type: "first_price" or "second_price".
  • biddable_targeting_types: add "audience", "category" and so on for non-keyword units.
  • aggregate_geo_labels: long-tail buckets ("others", "rest of world") that must never be a target.
  • trading-day hours (PlatformProfile.trading_day_start_hour, trading_day_end_hour, funding_target_hour), if the defaults of 6, 18 and 22 do not fit.

Optional, and how the engine degrades without it. Measured by removing feeds from the synthetic dataset and re-running (seed 7):

12What was measured

Every evaluation in this repository is on synthetic data with planted events. This section is the recovery check behind the worked example. The paper (docs/paper/cpm-lineage-paper.pdf) reports a wider set of synthetic experiments from docs/paper/experiments.py: 30 seeds, a world with no planted events, a dose-response sweep, a second platform, and the cases where detection failed or the plan over-acted.

Setup. adapters.synthetic.generate(seed) builds a 90-day account with nine campaigns and plants five events: a CPM spike on one keyword, an availability drop for one product in one geo, a budget-capped winner, a bleeding competitor keyword, and two own campaigns bidding the same keyword. The seed changes the noise, not the events.

Command. python docs/guide/worked_example.py --eval 20

Result. With the default configuration, on seeds 0 to 19:

What this does and does not show. It shows that the feeds reach the right rules and that the rules fire on events of the planted size under seeded noise. The generator and the defaults were written together, the events are large (a 1.6 times CPM step, a ROAS of 0.35), and the data contains no unplanted problems of the kinds the rules look for. So this is a plumbing check. It says nothing about detection rates, false alarms or the right thresholds on a real account.

13Limits

  • The thresholds are judgment defaults. All 1,040 config fields are starting points. None was fitted to or validated on real account data in this repository. docs/thresholds.md lists every one.
  • The three money estimators are not reconciled. The stake (a rule of thumb), the fitted saturation curve and the finite-difference marginal ROAS answer slightly different questions. The example raise carries a stake of 476,249, an expected value of 19,996 a day at average ROAS 6.30, and a pooled-curve marginal ROAS of 2.80, which would put the same 3,175 a day at about 8,900. Every sized number carries an estimator label. Never add or average across labels.
  • Attribution is observational, not causal. See step 2.
  • The self-floor check assumes a second-price auction. It flags a keyword where your own next campaign beneath you sets your price. It runs only when the profile says second_price and bids are quoted as CPM, and it has not been verified against billed prices.
  • The saturation curve is correlational. It has no demand controls, same-day attribution only, an in-sample fit statistic and no confidence interval. It cannot be fitted for an entity that always spends exactly its cap.
  • Window averages lag. A step change late in a window shows up diluted.
  • The finite-difference marginal reads day-of-week variation as spend response. It is crude by design.
  • Expected value is not recomputed after the staircase trim. See step 5.
  • Outcome labels are before-and-after reads adjusted for the market tide. They are not experiments. The holdout group is drawn and reported, but the library does not compute a holdout-versus-treated comparison for you.
  • Money thresholds assume one scale. Set currency_unit if a material campaign does not spend thousands a day. diagnose() adds a note when the data sits far below the scale; plan() does not.

15FAQ

Does it change anything on my ad platform?

No. It reads records and returns dataclasses. Nothing is applied. ledger.mark(...) records that a person applied a card; it does not apply it.

Does it need clicks?

No. The identity uses impressions, units, sales and spend. Clicks are optional.

Do I need all the feeds?

No. Only delivery rows are required. The degradation table in section 10 shows what each missing feed turns off.

Why did a problem I know about get no card?

Check four things in this order. The entity may be in the holdout (Plan.holdout). The finding may exist in diagnose() with no matching rule in the profile you ran. The card may be in Plan.actions with status vetoed or superseded and a reason. Or the money may be under a threshold because currency_unit is not set for your scale.

Why does diagnose() show a finding that plan() does not act on, or the reverse?

They are separate reads. diagnose() reports events. plan() runs rule families that have their own triggers. A finding is evidence; a card is a proposal.

Why is ROAS different in two places?

Entity ROAS in diagnose() and plan() counts EngineConfig.indirect_sales_weight of indirect sales (1.0 by default). The identity table and the ledger read direct sales only. The report labels the basis.

How do I make a plan reproducible?

It already is. The clock is the latest date in the data, and the plan id is a hash of the date, the rule profile, the options and the card ids. Pass plan_key= if every run should be a new plan. For the ledger, pass ActionLedger(clock=...) and today= so no method reads the wall clock.

Can I use only part of it?

Yes. Every subsystem is a set of pure functions. identity.decompose_rows needs only rows and a window pair.

How do I add a rule?

A family is a function from ActionContext to a list of cards built with make_card. Add it to the actions.families.FAMILY_RULES dict before calling plan(), and give its family name a row in PipelineConfig.priority so the conflict table knows where it ranks (an unlisted family ranks 0).

What does it not do?

  • It does not forecast. There is no demand model and no seasonality model.
  • It does not run experiments or compute lift. The holdout is drawn; analysing it is up to you.
  • It does not estimate causal effects. Attribution and outcome labels are observational.
  • It does not optimise bids continuously or pace budgets intraday. It reviews a window and proposes discrete moves.
  • It does not handle creative, audiences as a modelled lever, or cross-platform budget allocation.
  • It does not fetch data. There is no API client for any platform.
  • It does not know your margin. You state breakeven ROAS and the lifetime-value multiplier.
  • It has not been validated on real accounts in this repository.
